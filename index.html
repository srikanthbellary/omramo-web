Why it exists

No narration. No video. No autoplay. The page turns only when the reader presses the button, because the child reading the words is the whole point.

  • The page is a place

    Every spread is one continuous scene running under both panes. The words sit in the calm part of the picture: sky, twilight, sea haze.

  • Each story has its own light

    A bright river morning. A twilight forest. A pale sea. The buttons stay the same, so a child always knows where they are.

  • Pictures serve the words

    Illustration and tap-to-see pictures are there to pull a child into the text, and to send them back to it. Nothing plays by itself.

The library

A map that fills in as they read.

Every story has its pin. Finish one and it joins the shelf; meet a character and they become a friend. Everything a child earns comes from reading.

  • A page counts when it is turned after reading.
  • A day counts when at least one page is read.
  • No points, no coins, no timers.

Tap to see

Tap a word. See it. Come straight back.

When a story mentions something a child has never seen, the moment doesn’t pass. The picture opens on the facing pane, and the text never moves.

One picture answer at a time.

  1. Tap the marked word

    It lights up, and the line being read is held with a marker while the rest of the page dims.

  2. See it on the facing pane

    One picture, one line to read, one short culture note. No onward links to wander down.

  3. One button home

    “Back to my line” is the only way out, and it goes exactly where it says.

Back at the very same line.

The card closes, the marker still shows the sentence, and a small message confirms it: “You are back at your line.” The word is now marked as seen, and it can be tapped again.

The page cannot be turned while a card is open, so a picture is never a way to skip the reading.

Character cards

Meet who you’re reading about.

Tap a name with a dotted underline and the character steps onto the facing pane: who they are, a few traits, and one short description. Then one button, back to the line.

A character becomes a “friend you have met” the first time a child meets them in the text.

Folded mode

Folded, it’s the same story.

Close the Duo and the page settles onto one screen: picture above, words below, the same round button to turn the page.

  • Your place carries across the fold. Page 5 of 14 open is page 5 of 14 folded.
  • Reading position is remembered between sittings, down to the paragraph.

For grown-ups

Pages read, not screen time.

The parent view sits behind a grown-up gate and reports one thing: reading. There is no timer and no usage clock anywhere in the app.

  • Pages read over the last seven days.
  • Stories finished and cultures visited.
  • Progress at each level, with the story being read now.

Numbers on the screen are sample data.

Privacy by design

Built to collect nothing.

  • No ads

    Not in the stories, not in the library, not anywhere.

  • No accounts

    No sign-in and no child profile to create.

  • No tracking

    No analytics and no third-party code in the app.
