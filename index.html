Omramo is a reader built for a phone that folds. Two panes, one spine, a new chapter every week. The screen bends in the middle, so the story is bound there.

01 · The fold

Two pages, one spine.

The mark is a book seen from above. OM sits on the left page and MO on the right, the same two letters turned around. RA holds the spine, the way a title does on a real one. Pushed together, the six letters sit on a single baseline and read Omramo. Pulled apart, you are looking at a spread.

Nothing here is typed. Each letter is drawn on a 20 unit grid from stems, wedges and ellipse arcs, so the mark scales from a favicon to a wall without a font on the machine.

02 · The reader

Built for a screen that folds.

iPhone Duo opens like a hardcover. Omramo puts the illustration on the left pane and the text on the right, and a page turn moves both together. You do not scroll through a chapter. You turn it, and the crease in the glass becomes the gutter of the book.

Body text in the reader is Telugu. The controls around it, and everything on this site, stay in Latin, so the script is never borrowed as decoration.

03 · Paper and ink

One paper, one ink, one color that moves.

The whole system is a sheet and what is printed on it. Night is not a second brand; it is the same paper with the lamp off.

Paper

#F5EFE3 · night #1A1713

Every surface. Warm, matte, never white. Cards and pages are the same paper, one shade deeper.

Ink

#1E1A16 · night #EFE6D6

Text, marks, rules. One ink at three strengths, and nothing else is ever black.

Saffron

#E07A1F · same at night

The only color that acts. Buttons, focus rings, the underline you are about to click. Never a fill for the mark.
